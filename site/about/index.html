01 Project background

What it is.

Autognose (自知 — "knowing oneself") is a research project into self-aware knowledge layers for retrieval-augmented systems and agents.

Retrieval systems answer. They rarely say how well-founded the answer is. Autognose keeps what a system knows about its own knowledge: where a piece came from, whether it was ever checked, whether it still holds, and how confident the system is entitled to be. Its job is to decide, not to generate: given a question and what was retrieved for it — answer, retrieve more, ask, or decline. A decision made honestly is what we mean by self-aware.

The project started in September 2026 with a survey of 40+ works on self-aware knowledge systems, and a deliberately uncomfortable finding: the components exist in separate systems, but no assembled system demonstrates them together. That assembly is the hypothesis — registered as C-01, and treated as a hypothesis, not a finding.

What it is not

Not a RAG framework. Keep your retriever; Autognose does not replace it and does not care which one you use.

Not a vector database. No index, no storage engine.

Not an LLM training project. No fine-tuning required.

Not an AGI claim. No consciousness, no sentience. Self-aware means a system can report the state of its own knowledge — nothing more.

Not usable yet. No code, no benchmark, no demo, no release. This site is not a preview of one.

02 Organization

Who runs it.

Autognose is developed under Oasis AI Lab, on GitHub, as open core under the MIT license (© 2026 CeaserZhao).

The project is run on a strict protocol, written down in AGENT.md: a boundary report before action, one minimal next step at a time, and any decision that belongs to the team waits for the team. Documents carry status labels — proposed, open, recorded — and the public documentation is written in English while internal decision records remain in Chinese. That language split is itself a registered open question (OD-7).

03 Contributing

A case,
not a patch.

Pull requests would be premature until the schema is fixed — a statement of sequence, not a closed door. Here is what actually helps right now.

W·1

Bring failure cases.

If you have watched a retrieval system answer confidently where the evidence was thin, or could not tell whether a passage was still true — open an issue with the case. Failure cases are what we are shortest on.

Open an issue →
W·2

Challenge the documents.

The scope, the experiment plan, and the claim register are proposals awaiting review. If a status label is wrong or a baseline is missing, that is a contribution. Comment on the files or in issues.

W·3

Tell us if the wedge is wrong.

The assumption that open-source developers who run retrieval stacks recognise this problem comes from reading, not from talking to you. Prove it wrong early — that is the cheapest possible failure.

W·4

Code — later, by design.

The knowledge-state field set (OD-2) determines the public API. Writing code before that decision would make the site's promises untrue. When the schema is decided, the scaffold and the experiment plan are the entry points.
