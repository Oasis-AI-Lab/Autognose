Research

The agenda is organised around four questions. Every claim the project makes is registered with its evidence status — including the ones that weaken the core bet.

01 Research directions

Four questions.

RQ·01

Representation — what is the minimal epistemic-state representation that changes behaviour?

The core bet of the project. If runtime signals suffice — no persistent self-state layer — the "explicit state" hypothesis is weakened, and the architecture shrinks. Tested by experiments E1 (representation ablation) and E2 (representation swap).

Hypothesis · contested
RQ·02

Acquisition — where do epistemic labels legitimately come from?

Self-knowledge needs labels: sufficient, stale, conflicting. Derived automatically, human-written, or synthesised? Synthetic labels bootstrap evaluation fast and carry a documented circularity risk — three bottom lines are recorded as harness requirements.

Accepted decision · validity risk documented
RQ·03

Metacognitive control — when does self-knowledge change action selection for the better?

An assessment is worthless if it does not change what happens next. Tested against a score-only baseline: does the action vocabulary beat a calibrated confidence score, in which regimes, at what cost?

Empirical question
RQ·04

Evaluation — how do we distinguish useful self-modeling from plausible self-description?

Assessment quality and downstream utility are reported separately, always. A system producing convincing self-descriptions is not necessarily self-aware in any operationally useful sense.

Standing method requirement
